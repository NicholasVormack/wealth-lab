Building Your Automation System

Step 1: Map Your Weekly Workflow

Write down everything you do in a typical week:

  • Write articles
  • Post on social media
  • Reply to comments
  • Update micro-tools
  • Check analytics
  • Send emails

Step 2: Identify Repetitive Tasks

Mark anything you do the same way every time:

  • Same social media platforms
  • Same posting times
  • Same email templates
  • Same file naming conventions

Step 3: Automate One Thing at a Time

Don't automate everything at once. Start with one:

Week 1: Set up Buffer free tier to schedule social posts Week 2: Set up Mailchimp automated welcome email Week 3: Set up Google Forms for lead capture Week 4: Set up UptimeRobot for site monitoring

Example: Automated Content Pipeline

Write article (Monday)
    ↓
Save to Google Drive (automatic)
    ↓
Schedule on Buffer (Monday, for rest of week)
    ↓
Post to Medium + Dev.to (Tuesday)
    ↓
Share on Reddit + X (Wednesday–Friday)
    ↓
Check analytics (Sunday)

This entire pipeline runs on free tools with minimal manual intervention.

What Not to Automate

  • Your voice: Don't auto-generate all content. People can tell.
  • Customer support: Respond personally to emails and comments.
  • Strategy: Don't let tools decide your niche or pricing.
  • Quality control: Always review before publishing.

Free vs Paid: When to Upgrade

Stay free until you're making $1K+/month consistently. Then consider:

  • Buffer paid ($6/month): More social accounts, advanced analytics
  • MailerLite paid ($10/month): More subscribers, advanced automation
  • Custom domain ($12/year): Professional appearance

Until then, free tiers are more than enough.

Bottom Line

Automation multiplies your time. Start with one free tool, automate one repetitive task, and build from there. The goal is to spend less time on mechanics and more time on creating and connecting.
