How the math works

Affiliate income is commission per customer multiplied by customers. What changes between low-ticket and high-ticket offers is how many customers you need before the number matters:

Commission per customer = product price × (commission % ÷ 100)
Customers for $1,000 = $1,000 ÷ commission per customer
Customers for $5,000 = $5,000 ÷ commission per customer

With a recurring program, that commission arrives every month the customer stays, so "customers" means active customers rather than total signups. That is the appeal of a high-ticket recurring offer: three customers at $400 a month is $1,200 before you have written a second article.

Nothing in this formula says high-ticket is easier. It says the arithmetic is more forgiving, and it says where your effort has to go: if you need three customers, you can write to twenty people. If you need four hundred, you need distribution, not outreach.

What the outreach has to do

A cold message has one job: earn a reply. It is not the place to explain the product's feature list, and it is certainly not the place to claim a result you cannot show. Three things make a message worth answering:

  • A specific reason you are writing to this person. "I saw you run a bookkeeping service for restaurants and your pricing page mentions..." beats "I love your content".
  • A plain description of what the product does for someone in their position. One sentence, no adjectives.
  • An easy way to decline. "No pressure either way" is not weakness; it is what keeps the reply rate up and the list clean.

Follow up once, a week later, then stop. Two messages is persistence; five is spam, and it is also how affiliate accounts get terminated. Read each program's terms on unsolicited email before sending anything — some prohibit it, and the rules on disclosure apply whether the message is a DM or an article.

What this cannot do

The generator writes text; it does not know whether your offer is good, whether the person you are writing to has a reason to care, or whether the program allows the approach. It also cannot verify the commission rate, so confirm it with the program. Treat every script as a first draft you edit until it sounds like a person who has actually used the product.

Frequently asked questions

Is cold outreach allowed by affiliate programs?

Often for direct one-to-one messages, rarely for bulk unsolicited email. Rules differ by program and by jurisdiction, so read the terms and the anti-spam law that applies where the recipient lives. When in doubt, do not send.

Do I need an audience to start?

No. With a high commission you can start with a short list of people who plausibly need the product. What you cannot skip is relevance — a generic message to a stranger is spam regardless of the commission.

How many messages before I get a reply?

There is no reliable number, and anyone quoting one is guessing. Track your own send and reply counts in a spreadsheet, and change the message rather than the volume when replies are zero.

Do I have to disclose the affiliate link?

Yes, in most jurisdictions, and in practice everywhere: a short line like "that's my affiliate link" is enough. Hiding it risks the account and the relationship. See the affiliate disclosure for how this site handles it.

Should I use the same message for everyone?

No. The first line has to be about them, and that cannot be templated. Use the script for the structure — reason, product, link, out — and rewrite the opening sentence for every recipient.
