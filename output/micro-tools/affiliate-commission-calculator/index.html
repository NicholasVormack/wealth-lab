How the math works

Affiliate income has three inputs and one multiplier. Traffic becomes clicks, clicks become sales, sales pay commission, and recurring programs keep paying for as long as the customer stays:

Sales per month = visitors × (conversion rate ÷ 100)
Commission per month = sales × commission per sale × recurring months
Annual figure shown = commission per month × 12

Conversion rate here means sales per visitor to the offer, not sales per visitor to your site. If 1,000 people read a post and 200 click your link, only those 200 count. Mixing the two is the single most common way these projections end up ten times too high.

The recurring months field needs care. With a one-time commission, leave it at 1 and the result is exactly this month's payout. With a recurring program, the number is your steady state: the monthly commission once enough customers have accumulated. You do not get it in month one, because a customer who joins today has only paid one month.

Worked example

1,000 visitors a month, 3% converting, $25 average commission, one-time payout:

The same traffic through a program that pays $25 a month for 12 months gives $9,000 a month at steady state — but only after a year of consistent traffic, and only if none of those customers cancel. In year one the actual total is far lower, because the first customers have been paying for a few months at most. The $9,000 is a run rate, not a first-year total.

What the number leaves out

  • Churn. The formula assumes nobody cancels. If half of your referrals leave within the year, halve the recurring figure.
  • Refunds and holdbacks. Most programs reverse commissions on refunds and hold payouts for 30 to 90 days. Subtract the refund rate from sales before trusting the total.
  • Attribution. A sale is only yours if the cookie or tracking still recognises the visitor. Privacy settings and multiple devices break that chain.
  • Tax and fees. Payout fees, currency conversion and income tax all come out of the number shown.
  • Traffic quality. Visitors who arrive looking for something else convert near zero no matter what the page says.

How to pick the inputs

Start with 1% to 2% conversion for cold blog or video traffic and replace it with your measured figure as soon as you have one. Use the lowest commission the program actually pays, not the headline maximum, and set recurring months to the minimum term the program guarantees rather than "forever". A projection built from cautious inputs survives contact with a dashboard; one built from the best case does not.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 3% conversion rate realistic?

For warm traffic to a relevant offer, occasionally. For cold traffic to a general offer, usually not. Use your own data as soon as you have 100 clicks — below that the sample is too small to mean anything.

Why is my first month lower than the calculator?

Because recurring commissions accumulate. Set recurring months to 1 to see a single month's payout, or accept that the recurring figure is where you land after roughly that many months, not today.

Should I subtract refunds?

Yes, and the simplest way is to reduce sales by your refund rate before entering the numbers. A 10% refund rate turns 30 sales into 27.

Does the commission include the platform's cut?

No. Enter the amount the affiliate program pays you. If a network takes a slice before paying, use the net figure.

What about recurring programs that pay forever?

Treat "forever" as the period you can defend. Customers cancel, products get discontinued and programs change terms. Modelling 12 or 24 months is more honest than modelling infinity.
