# LinkedIn Organic for B2B — No Ads, No Budget

LinkedIn is unusual: the people who control budgets use it, and organic reach for text posts is still high compared with other networks. It is the best free channel for selling to businesses, and the easiest one to do badly.

## The profile is the landing page

Before posting anything, fix the profile. Visitors decide whether to read your post based on who you appear to be.

- **Headline** — state the outcome you produce, not your job title. "I fix onboarding emails that lose signups" beats "Growth Consultant".
- **Banner and photo** — a real photo and a legible banner. Both take ten minutes.
- **About section** — first two lines are all that shows before the cutoff. Make them a specific claim, not a mission statement.
- **Featured** — pin one piece of work, one tool, or one case study.

Read your headline back as a stranger: can they tell what you produce, or only what your job is called? That crude check catches the worst offenders.

## Choose two topics and stay on them

Accounts that post about everything are forgettable. Pick two related themes you can speak about credibly and for months.

A workable split:

- **Practical how-to** — the mechanics of a problem your buyer has
- **Evidence and results** — numbers from your own work, including the failures

Avoid motivational content and vague observations about "the future of work". Both are crowded and neither attracts buyers.

## Post something concrete three times a week

Frequency matters less than whether each post contains something a reader could act on. Three posts a week is sustainable; daily posting usually collapses into filler.

Formats that work for B2B:

- A specific number with the story behind it
- A before-and-after of a real piece of work
- A short teardown of a common mistake, with the fix
- A plain-text list of what you would do in a given situation

Keep the first two lines doing the work. On mobile, that is all that appears before "see more".

## Comment more than you post

This is the highest-leverage free activity on the platform and the one most people skip. Thoughtful comments on posts your buyers already read put you in front of their audience without needing your own reach.

Ten substantive comments a day, each two or three sentences that add something, outperforms most posting schedules. "Great post" does nothing.

## Outreach that does not read like spam

Connection requests with a pitch attached are ignored. The sequence that works:

1. Connect with no message, or a one-line reason you are connecting
2. Engage with their content for a week or two
3. Send a short message about something specific you noticed
4. Only then, if relevant, mention what you do

Volume is not the lever here; precision is. Thirty well-chosen people beat three hundred scraped names, and the latter risks account restrictions.

## What to measure

Ignore impressions. Track:

- **Profile views** — are the right people looking?
- **Conversations started** — how many replies did you get?
- **Calls booked** — the only number that predicts revenue

If profile views rise but conversations do not, the profile claim is unclear. If conversations happen but nothing closes, the offer is the problem, not the marketing.

## Bottom line

A sharp profile, two topics, three concrete posts a week, and daily comments on other people's posts. That is the whole free playbook. It takes about thirty minutes a day and works slowly and reliably.
